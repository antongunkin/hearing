# Monetization Economics for a Hearing-Aid Content/Review Website (2025-2026)

Research date: 2026-11-26. US/English market. Each finding is marked **[Confirmed]** (primary or official source), **[Third-party]** (aggregator or affiliate directory, not the brand's own terms), or **[Estimate]** (modeled or vendor-claimed). Most affiliate terms in this niche are published only inside network dashboards (Impact, ShareASale/Awin, FlexOffers), so public figures come mostly from aggregators and often conflict. Treat every affiliate rate below as something to check after approval.

## 1. Affiliate programs: commissions, AOV, cookies, networks

### Takeaway
Direct-to-consumer OTC and online hearing aid brands pay well per sale: roughly $88-$120 flat, or 12-15% of a $300-$2,000+ order. Some programs pay only per lead ($8-$11). Amazon pays about 1% on Health & Personal Care, which is negligible. Public terms conflict a lot, so the model should use ranges.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- **Modeling payout per sale:** Lexie about $120, Eargo about $88 (only on orders over $1,000), MDHearing 15% of a pair priced roughly $300-$1,500, so roughly $45-$225. A reasonable blended figure is **$60-$120 per OTC/DTC sale**. Lead-only programs pay **$8-$11 per lead**.
- **Amazon:** 1% on a $150-$300 OTC device is $1.50-$3 per sale. Amazon is useful only as a fallback and for batteries and accessories, where it earns cents.
- **Network and brand ownership:** Global Market Insights names "LXE Hearing" among the top OTC players. This suggests the hearX (Lexie) and Eargo merger has consolidated those programs. Both programs' current terms should be checked, as they may have been unified.
- **Approval:** These programs usually require an established content site. Several, such as Eargo with its $1,000 minimum, exclude low-value orders.

### Gaps
- None of these brands publishes official affiliate terms. None of the figures could be checked against a brand's own terms page.
- No affiliate program data was found for Nuheara (which went into administration in 2024 and was acquired by ELEHEAR; based on my background knowledge, not verified in this research), Sony OTC (CRE-C10/E10), Sennheiser All-Day Clear, or tinnitus products such as Lenire and Neosensory.
- No 2025-2026 average order values were found for individual brands. The MarkeTrak median OTC price paid is about $150 (see Section 5).
- No affiliate rates were found for battery and accessory retailers such as ZipHearing or batteries.com.

## 2. Lead generation: CPL/CPA from clinics and networks; pay-per-call

### Takeaway
Publisher-facing lead programs (hear.com, Jabra Enhance, Eargo CashPay, ListenClear) pay only **$8-$11 per lead**. Clinics, however, report spending **$65-$600 per lead** and **$180-$1,000 per acquired patient** on their own marketing. That gap means a direct clinic or local lead-sale model could pay far more than network CPL. No public hearing-specific pay-per-call rates were found.

### Cited Findings
- **Network CPL offered to publishers:**
  - hear.com: $10-$11.25 per lead. [LinkClicky](https://linkclicky.com/affiliate-program/hear-com/)
  - Jabra Enhance: $10 per lead. [LinkClicky](https://linkclicky.com/affiliate-program/jabra-enhance/)
  - Eargo: $8 per CashPay lead. [FlexOffers](https://www.flexoffers.com/affiliate-programs/eargo-affiliate-program/)
  - ListenClear: $8 per lead. [FlexOffers](https://www.flexoffers.com/affiliate-programs/listenclear-hearing-aids-affiliate-program)
  - All of these are **[Third-party]**.
- **Clinic-side acquisition costs:**
  - Clinics commonly spend "upwards of $400-$600" per new lead. This is a vendor claim from a 2017 blog **[Estimate]**. [SHOEBOX](https://www.shoebox.md/hearing-care-industry-lead-generation-programs/)
  - Average cost to acquire a new audiology patient is $180-$420. The source warns that practices track CPL, not cost per acquired patient **[Estimate, agency]**. [RAD Audiology](https://radaudiology.com/patient-acquisition-cost-in-audiology-what-you-should-be-paying-per-new-hearing-evaluation/)
  - Hearing Review is cited as putting lead generation at $400-$1,000 per new patient **[Secondary citation]**. [aimarketer.com](https://aimarketer.com/blog/audiology-marketing-agency-pricing)
  - A worked example shows $12,000 spent for 100 leads, a CPL of $120, with a break-even CPL of about $590 **[Estimate, agency model]**. [Ear Level Marketing](https://earlevelmarketing.com/b/the-hidden-math-behind-hearing-care-growth-stop-guessing-start-scaling)
  - Derived Google Ads CPL for audiology is $65-$100. This is modeled and Australian **[Estimate]**. [Medical Marketing Group AU](https://www.medicalmarketinggroup.com.au/healthcare-google-ads-benchmarks/audiology-hearing/)
- **Pay-per-call:**
  - No published hearing-aid per-call payout was found.
  - A hearing-industry source says phone callers convert "10x" more than digital leads. [Hearing Health Matters](https://hearinghealthmatters.org/hearinprivatepractice/2020/google-call-only-ads/)
  - For comparison, a non-hearing pay-per-call example (debt relief) pays $55-$120 per call. [Authority Hacker](https://www.authorityhacker.com/pay-per-call-affiliate-programs/)

### Inferences
- **Scale-dependent model:** At low scale, use **$8-$11 per lead** from networks. At scale, a "find a clinic" or audiologist directory selling exclusive local leads or booked appointments could plausibly charge **$50-$150 per lead** or **$100-$300 per booked hearing test**. This is unconfirmed and derived from clinic CPL and CPA benchmarks of $65-$600.
- **Calls:** Pay-per-call for hearing would plausibly fall between web CPL and clinic CPA. No figure could be sourced.

### Gaps
- No public CPL or CPA figures were found for Amplifon, HearUSA, TruHearing, ZipHearing or Miracle-Ear partner programs. TruHearing and HearUSA mainly get members through health-plan benefits. Their publisher programs, if any, are not public.
- No hearing-specific pay-per-call rates were found on OfferVault, Ringba or Invoca-style networks.
- No US lead-to-appointment or appointment-to-sale conversion benchmarks were found.

## 3. Display advertising: RPMs, network thresholds, CPCs

### Takeaway
Health/wellness display RPM on premium networks is estimated at **about $10-$45 per 1,000 pageviews**, with Ezoic and AdSense lower. Entry thresholds dropped in 2025: Raptive now accepts 25K pageviews and Mediavine Journey 1K premium sessions. Mediavine main still requires 50K sessions. Hearing-aid keywords cost advertisers about **$4-$8+ per click**, which signals commercial value.

### Cited Findings
**Raptive (formerly AdThrive)**
- On Oct 16, 2025, Raptive cut its minimum from 100,000 to 25,000 monthly pageviews, effective immediately for new applications **[Confirmed via trade press]**. [SEJ, Oct 20, 2025](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/raptive-drops-traffic-requirement-by-75-to-25000-views/558780/); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/raptive-drops-pageview-requirement-to-25-000-monthly-visits/)
- Sites with 25K-99,999 pageviews need at least 50% of traffic from the US, UK, CA, AU or NZ. Sites with 100K+ need at least 40%.
- The Rise tier was merged into Insider.
- Publishers must also meet original-content and setup standards.

**Mediavine**
- The main program requires 50,000 sessions per month in Google Analytics, roughly 60,000 pageviews **[Confirmed, Mediavine]**. [Mediavine requirements](https://www.mediavine.com/?p=19304); [Create and Go](https://createandgo.com/mediavine-requirements)
- **Journey by Mediavine:** official pages now say 1,000 premium (tier-1) sessions per 30 days, and Grow is optional **[Confirmed, Mediavine help]**. [Mediavine help](https://help.mediavine.com/what-does-it-take-to-get-approved-by-mediavine); [Mediavine blog](https://www.mediavine.com/?p=85311)
- The older 10K-session figure in third-party guides is outdated.

**Ezoic**
- A competitor blog claims Ezoic raised its minimum from 10K to 250K monthly pageviews in Feb 2026 and locked out about 90% of small publishers **[Unverified; single, competitor source, internally inconsistent]**. [Newor Media](https://newormedia.com/blog/?p=1430)
- Other sources say Ezoic has no minimum, or has an "Access Now" program or a limited Incubator. [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/is-your-site-finally-ready-the-new-math-behind-premium-ad-network-approvals/)

### Inferences
- **Planning RPM:** For modeling, use about **$5-$12 on AdSense, $15-$30 on Mediavine or Raptive, and up to $35+** for a high-intent, US-heavy hearing site. Older (55+) audiences tend to view more pages on desktop, which can help RPM. That effect is not sourced.
- **Network path:** Start with Journey at 1K sessions. Move to Raptive at 25K pageviews, provided at least 50% of traffic is tier-1, which a US site will easily meet. Mediavine main becomes an option at 50K sessions.
- **Display versus affiliate:** CPCs of $4-$15 on commercial hearing terms suggest that on commercial pages, display ads earn far less per visitor than affiliate or lead links. Display works best as the monetization for informational pages.

### Gaps
- No official Mediavine or Raptive health-niche RPM data was found, and no senior-audience RPM data was found.
- No current US Keyword Planner or Semrush CPC was found for "hearing aids", "OTC hearing aids" or "best hearing aids". The figures above are Australian or category averages.
- The Ezoic 250K threshold could not be confirmed.

## 4. Direct sponsorships, sponsored reviews, newsletter sponsorship

### Takeaway
No public rate cards were found for hearing-aid manufacturer sponsored reviews. Newsletter sponsorship CPMs for health audiences are estimated at **$15-$60 per 1,000 sends or opens**, with a typical figure around $25-$45. No benchmark was found specific to readers aged 50+.

### Cited Findings
- Health/wellness newsletter CPM estimates conflict:
  - $40-$100 per thousand
  - $15-$40, typical $25
  - $28-$60, median about $42
  - "Health" base CPM of $45
  - Some sources say $10-$20
  - All are **[Estimate, vendor blogs/calculators]**. [Sponsorships.ai](https://sponsorships.ai/blog/newsletter-sponsorship-rates); [SponsorCal](https://sponsorcal.com/blog/newsletter-sponsorship-pricing); [Stroby](https://stroby.ai/blog/newsletter-cpm-benchmarks-2026); [SenderCircle](https://sendercircle.com/guides/health-wellness-newsletter-advertising-rates); [SenderCircle healthcare](https://sendercircle.com/guides/healthcare-newsletter-advertising-rates)

## 5. Market context: market size, OTC growth, prices, demographics, search demand

### Takeaway
The US hearing aid market is about **4.4M units per year**. Revenue estimates range widely, from $1.8B to $5.5B. OTC is still a small slice, roughly **$270M in 2025** and about 12% of current owners. OTC buyers are younger and pay far less: a **$150 median** versus **$1,560** for prescription aids. Overall adoption rose only from 38.4% to 39.1% after the 2022 OTC rule.

### Cited Findings
**Market size**
- The US dispenses about 4.4 million hearing aids per year, per HIA as cited. [Persistence Market Research](https://www.persistencemarketresearch.com/market-research/hearing-aids-market.asp)
- US market revenue estimates for 2025 conflict **[Estimate]**:
  - $1.8B ([IMARC](https://www.imarcgroup.com/united-states-hearing-aids-market))
  - $2.14B ([MarketsandMarkets](https://www.marketsandmarkets.com/Market-Reports/geography/hearing-aids-market/US))
  - $5.47B, with $4.94B in 2024 ([Fortune Business Insights](https://www.fortunebusinessinsights.com/u-s-hearing-aids-market-105653))

**OTC market**
- US OTC revenue is about $272.1M in 2025, with a 9.3% CAGR forecast for 2026-2035 **[Estimate]**. [GMI US OTC](https://www.gminsights.com/industry-analysis/us-otc-hearing-aids-market)
- Global OTC is $480M in 2025 with a 16% CAGR **[Estimate]**. [GMI global OTC](https://www.gminsights.com/industry-analysis/otc-hearing-aids-market)
- In the US, WS Audiology holds more than 27.4% share. The top 5 (GN, LXE Hearing, WSA, Lucid, Starkey) hold 76.4% **[Estimate]**. [GMI](https://www.gminsights.com/industry-analysis/us-otc-hearing-aids-market)
- OTC sales data is not publicly available. [AudiologyOnline](https://www.audiologyonline.com/articles/research-quick-takes-volume-10-29477)
- One estimate put the US OTC market at under 200,000 units, and the WSJ reported about 2% of adults with hearing difficulty had bought OTC (circa 2024). [Digital Trends](https://digitaltrends.com/home-theater/apple-airpods-pro-2-otc-hearing-aids-market-boost)

**MarkeTrak 2025 (HIA; n=4,993 adults with perceived hearing difficulty; 1,173 device owners; published Oct 2025) [Confirmed, peer-reviewed]**
- About 6% of adults with hearing difficulty own an OTC aid. 136 of 1,173 owners have OTC devices versus 1,008 with prescription aids.
- **Median price paid:** OTC $150, prescription $1,560.
- **Where OTC aids were bought:**
  - General retailer in-store (e.g., Costco): 20%
  - Brand website: 15%
  - General retailer website: 11%
  - Online-only retailer such as Amazon or eBay: 11%
  - Clinic: 6%
  - Pharmacy website: 2%
- **Age:** OTC owners have a mean age of 52 (median 58). Prescription owners have a mean of 70 (median 73). All owners have a mean of 68 (median 72).
- Source: [PMC MarkeTrak 25 OTC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12638185/)
- Adoption rose from 30.2% (2015) to 38.4% (2022) to 39.1% (2025), with no substantial change since OTC. 70% of OTC owners are first-time buyers versus 58% for prescription. [PMC MarkeTrak 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12638186/); [Hearing Review](https://hearingreview.com/inside-hearing/research/marketrak-2025-hearing-aids-in-the-age-of-otcs-and-wearables)

**Apple effect**
- The FDA authorized AirPods Pro hearing aid software (Sept 2024). Amplifon shares fell up to 7.2% and Sonova, Demant and GN fell 2-4%. Analysts expect cannibalization of low-price OTC but not of prescription aids. [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-authorizes-first-over-counter-hearing-aid-software); [Inc/Reuters](https://www.inc.com/reuters/hearing-aid-makers-shares-slip-after-apple-air-pods-release.html)

**Search demand**
- "hearing aids" gets 14,800 searches per month in Australia. [MMG AU](https://www.medicalmarketinggroup.com.au/healthcare-google-ads-benchmarks/audiology-hearing/)
- The OTC rule itself (FDA final rule Aug 2022, effective Oct 2022) comes from background knowledge and is not re-sourced here.

### Inferences
- **Affiliate-addressable buyers:** OTC owners are younger (median 58) and buy online. About 26% buy from a brand or online retailer website, and more if general retailer websites are included. This is the most affiliate-addressable segment.
- **Lead-gen audience:** Prescription buyers (median 73) are best monetized through clinic leads, not checkout links.
- **Per-sale economics:** A $150 median OTC price means percentage-based commissions on cheap devices are small, about $15-$25 at 10-15%. Flat-fee programs (Lexie $120, Eargo $88) and premium DTC brands are much more valuable per conversion.
- **Search volume:** US search volume is likely several times the Australian 14.8K for "hearing aids". This is unverified.

### Gaps
- No official HIA 2024/2025 unit sales or average selling price was found. The often-quoted "$4,700 per pair" prescription figure was not re-verified, and the MarkeTrak $1,560 median is per aid and may be per-unit or per-purchase.
- No US search volumes were found.

## 6. Constraints: FTC, Google Ads healthcare policy, network medical-claim rules

### Takeaway
Affiliate links and sponsorships require clear disclosure under the FTC Endorsement Guides (revised June 2023). The 2024 Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule adds civil penalties of about $51,744 per violation for fake or AI-generated reviews. Google restricts healthcare ads, but no hearing-aid-specific ban was found. Medical devices are allowed in Shopping ads subject to local law.

### Cited Findings
- **Endorsement Guides:** revised June 2023. They expanded "endorser" to include fake reviewers. The guides are guidance, without force of law, but material connections must be disclosed clearly and conspicuously. [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/topics/truth-advertising/advertisement-endorsements)
- **Consumer Reviews Rule:** final Aug 2024. It bans fake reviews and testimonials, AI-generated fake reviews included, and allows civil penalties for knowing violations. The figure reported is $51,744 per violation, inflation-adjusted, with each review possibly counted separately. [DWT](https://www.dwt.com/insights/2024/08/ftc-finalizes-rule-banning-fake-consumer-reviews); [FTC Q&A](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers); [BBB Programs](https://bbbprograms.org/media/insights/blog/ftc-fake-reviews)
- **Google Ads healthcare policy:** some content is banned and some is restricted to certified advertisers. Advertisers must follow local law for each targeted location. The policy was reorganized in Dec 2024, with updates in Apr, Jul and Oct 2025. [Google Ads policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031); [Oct 2025 update](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16606361)
- **Merchant Center:** medical devices may be advertised in Shopping ads subject to country-specific law. [Google Merchant Center](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/6150151?hl=en)

### Inferences
- **Disclosure:** For a review site, put an affiliate disclosure near the top of each page, not only in the footer. Real hands-on testing claims must be true. Fabricated "tested by" claims or synthetic user reviews carry real penalty risk under the 2024 rule.
- **Health-content quality:** Health content falls under Google Search's YMYL quality standards (background knowledge). Expert (audiologist) authorship and review will likely affect both organic traffic and ad-network approval.
- **Medical claims:** Avoid unapproved medical claims. OTC devices are cleared only for perceived mild-to-moderate loss in adults 18 and older, and tinnitus "cure" claims are risky for FTC and network compliance.

### Gaps
- No network-specific rules were found on medical claims, such as Impact or Awin brand terms prohibiting "cure" language or PPC brand bidding. These normally appear in each program's terms after approval.
- No published Mediavine or Raptive content policies specific to health/medical sites were found.

### Appendix of inferences: illustrative revenue per 1,000 visitors (not sourced as a whole; built from the ranges above)

- 1.6 pageviews per visit
- Display RPM of $20 (Raptive/Mediavine mid-range health)
- 60% of traffic informational and 40% commercial
- 5% merchant click-through on commercial pages
- 2% conversion on DTC clicks at $90 blended per sale
- 1% lead-form conversion of all visitors at $10 per lead
