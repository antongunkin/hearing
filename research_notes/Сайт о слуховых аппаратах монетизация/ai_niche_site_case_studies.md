# Real-world outcomes of AI-automated niche/affiliate sites and niche health/product-review sites (2023-2026)

Research date: 2026-11-26. Evidence labels: **[DATA]** means a dataset or multi-site analysis, **[CASE]** means a single named case with numbers, **[ANECDOTAL]** means a forum, Reddit, or self-reported claim, and **[VENDOR]** means a source with a commercial interest in the conclusion.

Method note: Several primary pages could not be fetched. Search Engine Land returned 403, Flippa's H1 2026 report returned 403, and a Reddit mirror refused the connection. Where a figure comes only from a search-result summary and not from the full page, it is marked "(snippet-level)".

## 1. AI-generated niche sites after Google's 2023-2026 updates: what happened, and what share lost traffic

### Takeaway
The best-documented pattern is the "Mount AI" curve: scaled AI content grows fast for 6-12 months, then falls, often below its starting baseline. In the largest dataset found (220+ sites, May 2026), 54% of sites using AI content platforms lost 30% or more of peak traffic, and 39% lost 50% or more. Full deindexing hit about 2% of monetized content sites in March 2024. That figure is lower than the broader decay rate, but the penalty is near-total and recovery is rare.

### Cited Findings
**Causal "SEO heist" (late 2023) [CASE]**
- Marketer Jake Ward used AI to clone thousands of Exceljet articles for the startup Causal and promoted it as an "SEO heist that stole 3.6M total traffic." Google then applied a manual penalty. Ahrefs estimates show Causal's traffic fell from about 610,000 visits in early October 2023 to about 190,000 by mid-December 2023 — [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/ai-powered-seo-heist-copying-website-loses-significant-google-traffic/); [HubSpot](https://blog.hubspot.com/ai/seo-heist)
- An Ahrefs table cited in search results shows Causal's organic traffic going from 307,158 (2023) to 1,485 (2024), a 99.5% drop. This is snippet-level and the exact Ahrefs page was not verified — [Ahrefs blog](https://ahrefs.com/blog/the-great-diversification)

**Lily Ray dataset of AI content platform customers (published May 13, 2026) [DATA]**
- The dataset covers 220+ sites and subfolders that used AI content platforms, with Ahrefs data corroborated by Sistrix. 54% lost 30% or more of peak organic traffic, 39% lost 50% or more, and 22% lost 75% or more — [Lily Ray, Substack](https://lilyraynyc.substack.com/p/it-works-until-it-doesnt-ai-content-risks) (also on [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/it-works-until-it-doesnt-ai-content-strategies-that-backfire/574820/))
- Typical trajectory: organic pages grow fast for 6-12 months, traffic peaks about 3-6 months after the content peak, then declines within the following year, often below the prior baseline. Glenn Gabe calls this the "Mount AI" shape — [Lily Ray](https://lilyraynyc.substack.com/p/it-works-until-it-doesnt-ai-content-risks)
- Around January 20-21, 2026 there was an unconfirmed Google update. At least 40 of those sites (about 18%) declined 40-95% between January and April 2026. The impact was heaviest in B2B and on self-promotional listicles. Verticals in the case-study set included healthcare and consumer goods — [Lily Ray](https://lilyraynyc.substack.com/p/it-works-until-it-doesnt-ai-content-risks)
- Many brands featured in published AI-content "success" case studies later removed, redirected, or 410'd the featured pages, while the case studies themselves stayed live. This means published AI success stories are survivorship-biased and often outdated — [Lily Ray](https://lilyraynyc.substack.com/p/it-works-until-it-doesnt-ai-content-risks)
- Caveat: the dataset consists of AI content platform customers. It is not a random sample of all AI sites.

**March 2024 core update and spam update: deindexing wave [DATA, partly VENDOR]**
- Google said the March 2024 core update aimed to cut unhelpful, unoriginal content in results by 45%. It also launched the "scaled content abuse" spam policy, which applies whether pages are produced by automation, humans, or both — [Lily Ray](https://lilyraynyc.substack.com/p/it-works-until-it-doesnt-ai-content-risks); [SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/scaled-ai-content-often-fails-googles-crawl-economics-explain-why/)
- Originality.ai checked about 79k websites on Mediavine, Raptive, and Ezoic. 1,446 received manual actions (about 2%), with notices from March 5, 2024. In a sub-study of 14 deindexed sites, 7 had more than 90% AI-generated content. Originality.ai sells AI detection, the sub-sample is small, and the AI attribution rests on its own detector — [Originality.ai](https://originality.ai/blog/google-march-2024-manual-action-ai-update-list); [TechStartups](https://techstartups.com/2024/03/18/google-deindexed-2-of-sites-in-its-march-update-as-it-cracks-down-on-ai-generated-content-originality-ai-studies-find/)
- In an independent check, Ian Nuttall found 837 of 49,345 sites (1.7%) fully removed from search. SEJ called this consistent with Originality.ai's result — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/googles-march-2024-core-update-impact-hundreds-of-websites-deindexed/510981/)

**September 2023 Helpful Content Update (HCU) and recovery [DATA + CASE]**
- The HCU rolled out September 14-28, 2023. SISTRIX listed 250 losing domains — [Sistrix](https://www.sistrix.com/?p=146473); [SE Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-september-2023-helpful-content-completed-rolling-out-36127.html)
- Search Engine Land reported that thousands of blogs across niches were down as much as 90% [ANECDOTAL, aggregated] — [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/niche-blogging-new-google-reality-strategies-440595)
- Recovery: in spring 2024 there were still no HCU recoveries. Glenn Gabe tracked about 400 heavily hit sites; by November 2024, after the August 2024 core update, only 22% showed a lift of 20% or more. Most saw no lift, and Gabe called full recovery an "anomaly." Google said HCU recoveries may take much longer than other recoveries — [SE Roundtable, 22% report](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-helpful-content-update-recovery-data-38358.html); [SE Roundtable, no recoveries](https://seroundtable.com/no-hcu-yes-core-google-update-recoveries-37144.html); [SE Roundtable, longer recoveries](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-helpful-content-update-recoveries-much-longer-37317.html)
- HouseFresh, an independent air-purifier review site built on human first-hand testing, lost about 95% of its Google traffic after the September 2023 HCU (about 4,000 to about 200 daily visitors). Search Engine Land separately reported a 91% loss tied to the March 2024 update. Retro Dodo self-reported an 85-90% loss — [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/housefresh-achieves-notable-traffic-recovery-after-google-algorithm-impacts-2/); [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/review-site-google-traffic-affiliate-seo-content-440143); [HouseFresh](https://housefresh.com/how-google-decimated-housefresh/)
- Search Engine Land attributed the HouseFresh losses partly to brand signals: big generalist publishers such as The Spruce have significant brand search, while HouseFresh had "zero brand searches" — [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/review-site-google-traffic-affiliate-seo-content-440143)

**2025-2026 updates [DATA thin; mostly reporting]**
- Google began issuing manual actions specifically for scaled content abuse in June 2025 (snippet-level) — [SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/scaled-ai-content-often-fails-googles-crawl-economics-explain-why/)
- March 2026: a spam update targeting scaled content abuse came first, then the core update (March 27 to April 8, 2026, 12 days). On April 3, 2026, Glenn Gabe reported that sites built on AI-generated content were losing traffic. Assessments of volatility conflict, ranging from "moderate" to "most volatile on record" — [SE Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-march-2026-core-update-41121.html); [SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-confirms-march-2026-core-update-is-complete/571459/); [SimilarLabs](https://similarlabs.com/blog/google-march-2026-core-update-ai-content)
- A Brazilian agency blog headline claims a "71% drop for mass-produced AI content" in March 2026. No methodology was visible, so treat it as unverified — [Conversion.com.br](https://www.conversion.com.br/blog/google-core-update-marco-2026-impacto)
- Search results mention an August 2026 spam update. Its details were not retrieved — [SE Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-august-2026-spam-update-37920.html)

**Structural headwind: AI Overviews [DATA]**
- Ahrefs (February 2026 update) compared about 150k keywords with AI Overviews against 150k without, using December 2023 vs December 2025. With an AI Overview present, CTR at position 1 was 58% lower, at position 2 51% lower, and at position 10 19% lower. Ahrefs' April 2025 version had found 34.5% — [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-reduce-clicks-update/)
- Other estimates, as summarized by Ahrefs: Seer Interactive found organic CTR down 49-65%, Kevin Indig more than 50%, and Authoritas 47.5% — [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-reduce-clicks-update/)
- Ahrefs also reports that the average site's search traffic fell about 21% while its AI-referral traffic grew about 10x. Wix says LLMs still account for less than 1% of site traffic (snippet-level) — [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-traffic-increase/); [Wix](https://www.wix.com/blog/how-ai-search-impacts-websites)

### Inferences
- For a fully AI-automated site, the base rate is roughly a coin flip on losing half or more of peak traffic within about 12-18 months of peaking, plus a small (about 2%) but catastrophic chance of deindexing. Hearing aids are a health and medical-device topic (YMYL), which likely raises both risks.
- Human-made, first-hand testing sites such as HouseFresh were also crushed in 2023-2024. Quality alone did not protect small sites; brand signals and off-Google demand mattered. A new site with no brand searches starts in the most exposed position.
- Recovery from a site-wide quality demotion is slow and uncommon: about 22% partial recovery among tracked sites after more than a year, and about 2 years in the HouseFresh case. A plan should assume that a hit is not reversible on a 12-month horizon.
- AI Overviews cut clicks hardest on informational queries, which is the type a hearing-aid content site would target ("how do hearing aids work", "best hearing aids for tinnitus"). Expect lower traffic per ranking than pre-2024 benchmarks suggest.

### Gaps
- No representative, random-sample estimate exists of the share of all AI-generated sites that lost traffic. The Lily Ray and Originality.ai samples are each biased in different ways.
- The full Search Engine Land "SEO shortcuts gone wrong" case compilation could not be fetched (403).
- Quantified data on the 2025 (March, June, December) and August 2026 updates for AI sites was not found. Most of what exists is commentary.
- Originality.ai's 2025-2026 follow-up studies, Authority Hacker AI experiments, and Income School results were not found with numbers. The Authority Hacker survey page fetched contained only marketing copy, no survey data.

## 2. Typical timeline, revenue per 1,000 sessions, and exit valuations

### Takeaway
No rigorous 2024-2026 study measures the time from launch to 10k, 50k, or 100k monthly visits. Practitioner consensus is 3-6 months to first meaningful impressions and long-tail traffic, and 6-12+ months to meaningful traffic. Revenue per 1,000 sessions for health-related sites is roughly $15-30 from display ads and $40-80 from affiliate links in small samples, though one survey reports much higher affiliate averages. Exit multiples for content sites have fallen to about 2x annual profit (24x monthly on Empire Flippers in 2025; 2.32x annual average on Flippa in H1 2026), with lower deal volume and longer time to close.

### Cited Findings
**Timeline [ANECDOTAL / VENDOR]**
- Google has never confirmed a "sandbox." Ahrefs calls it an alleged filter — [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/google-sandbox/)
- Practitioner and vendor ranges: rankings stabilize around months 4-6, meaningful traffic growth comes in months 6-12, and the "sandbox" lasts 3-12 months depending on niche. Forum reports put real results at about 6 months and noticeable traffic at about 1 year — [Authority Hacker, how long to rank](https://www.authorityhacker.com/how-long-to-rank-on-google/); [Webscraft](https://webscraft.org/blog/pisochnitsya-google-yak-zrozumiti-scho-sayt-prohodit-testuvannya?lang=en); [BlackHatWorld thread](https://www.blackhatworld.com/posts/21308765/)
- A secondhand claim says only 1.74% of newly published pages reach Google's top 10 within a year, down from 5.7% in an earlier Ahrefs study. The original was not verified — [ClimbTheLadder](https://climbtheladder.com/how-long-does-it-take-to-get-organic-traffic-real-data/)
- Single-case data points [ANECDOTAL]: an r/juststart post (May 2024) titled "Automated AI SEO Content Site $0 to $3,674/m in 14 Months" (the post could not be fetched, so no follow-up on survival) — [r/juststart mirror](https://lr.us.psf.lt/r/juststart/comments/1cr0915/case_study_automated_ai_seo_content_site_0_to). A Niche Pursuits podcast guest (Casey Botticello) claims an AI-assisted site reached $21k+/month in under 12 months, and the host notes this is not typical — [Niche Pursuits](https://www.nichepursuits.com/casey-botticello/). An Indie Hackers post claims 0 to 100k visits in 5 months for an AI-tools site, but a later note says roughly half the traffic spike appeared to be bots — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/how-i-grew-my-ai-website-from-0-to-100k-visits-in-5-months-a-complete-breakdown-8489babd70)
- Niche Pursuits ran an "AI Website Challenge" with 6-month final results; the winner was the person with the most organic traffic after 6 months. The numbers were not retrieved — [Niche Pursuits](https://www.nichepursuits.com/build-a-new-ai-website-challenge/)
- Lily Ray's dataset shows AI sites peaking at 6-12 months and then declining. Fast early growth is therefore not evidence of durability — [Lily Ray](https://lilyraynyc.substack.com/p/it-works-until-it-doesnt-ai-content-risks)

**Revenue per 1,000 sessions [VENDOR / small samples]**
- Health and fitness: $15-30 display RPM and $40-80 affiliate RPM, from a 47-blog benchmark — [EarnifyHub](https://earnifyhub.com/blog/blog-monetization-benchmarks-revenue-per-1000-visitors)
- An Authority Hacker survey of 2,270 affiliate marketers (2025) reports an average affiliate site RPM of $149.76 across all niches (snippet-level; the fetched page did not show the survey). It is likely skewed by high-value niches — [Authority Hacker](https://www.authorityhacker.com/affiliate-marketing-survey/)
- Mediavine-level display (50k sessions/month threshold) earns about $20-40+ per 1,000 sessions according to an unsourced blog — [HostAdvice](https://hostadvice.com/blog/monetization/how-much-traffic-does-a-website-need-to-make-money/). Publift's niche range is $4-40 — [Publift](https://www.publift.com/calculator/rpm-calculator)

**Exit valuations [DATA, marketplace-reported]**
- Empire Flippers content sites (145 transactions):

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2023 | about 30x | about $175k | about 90% | about 94% | about 55 | about 70 |
| 2024 | about 27x | about $240k | about 85% | about 90% | about 72 | about 50 |

  Empire Flippers calls 2025 a buyer's market. Buyers are wary of single-channel Google traffic and AI risk, and smaller lifestyle sites were hit hardest. Sites convertible to SaaS, e-commerce, digital products, or newsletters are seen as lower risk — [Empire Flippers](https://empireflippers.com/you-can-sell-your-business-for-life-changing-money-in-2025-heres-the-secret/)
- Empire Flippers switched to annual multiples, effective September 8, 2026 per the search summary (snippet-level). 24x monthly equals about 2.0x annual — [Empire Flippers](https://empireflippers.com/switching-to-annual-multiples/)
- Flippa H1 2026: content sites averaged 2.32x annual profit, and the top quartile reached 4.68x annual (snippet-level; the report page returned 403) — [Flippa H1 2026 report](https://flippa.com/lps/insights-report-h12026)
- A third-party guide claims a 33.2x monthly average on Empire Flippers in 2025. This contradicts Empire Flippers' own 24x and has no source — [Organic Arbitrage](https://organicarbitrage.pages.dev/articles/revenue-multiple-valuation-seo-sites)
- FE International has a 2026 content-business multiples post, which was not read — [FE International](https://www.feinternational.com/blog/website-and-content-business-valuation-multiples-in-2026)

### Inferences
- Illustrative only: at 30k sessions/month and about $40-80 RPM, a site would make about $1.2k-2.4k/month. At about 2x annual profit (after costs), it would sell for about $25k-55k. This assumes a sustained profit history, typically 12+ months of stable traffic, which AI-heavy sites often lack because of the Mount AI curve.
- Buyers discount single-channel Google and AI-content sites. A site with a mostly AI content footprint and no brand signals may be hard to sell at all. A diversified asset (an email list, YouTube, a proprietary tool or data) is what keeps the exit option alive.
- Expect 12+ months before reaching typical display-network thresholds (about 50k sessions) for a new YMYL site, if it gets there at all. No reliable dataset supports a stated probability.

### Gaps
- No systematic data on time-to-10k, 50k, or 100k visits for new sites launched in 2024-2026.
- No hearing-aid-specific or medical-device-specific RPM or EPC data was found here.
- Motion Invest data was not found. Flippa's full H1 2026 report could not be read.

## 3. Hearing-aid and senior-health content sites and channels: public numbers and differentiators

### Takeaway
The visible winners in hearing-aid content are expert-led and first-hand. HearingTracker combines its own lab (HearAdvisor) with hands-on review and audiologist video. The large YouTube channels are run by practicing audiologists: Dr. Cliff Olson and Audiology Associates UK. No public revenue figures exist for any of them. Their moats are credentials, proprietary testing data, community (forums), and video. An AI-automated site can replicate none of these.

### Cited Findings
- **HearingTracker** says it has been independent since 2013. Its revenue comes from shop sales, advertising, and fixed fees manufacturers pay HearAdvisor for public lab testing. It states the fee "pays for the testing, never the result" and that it does not accept payments for positive reviews — [HearingTracker About](https://hearingtracker.com/about); [HearingTracker review process](https://www.hearingtracker.com/expert-vetting-our-hearing-aid-review-process)
- **HearAdvisor** (launched 2023, shares a founder with HearingTracker) runs a sound-treated lab with an acoustic manikin (KEMAR) and recreated ambisonic audio scenes. It produces a "SoundScore," which HearingTracker combines with hands-on wear testing — [Hearing Review](https://hearingreview.com/inside-hearing/industry-news/hearadvisor-launches-audio-lab-to-test-and-rate-hearing-aids); [HearingTracker](https://www.hearingtracker.com/news/hearadvisor-launches-audio-lab-to-test-and-rate-otc-and-prescription-hearing-aids)
- **HearingTracker YouTube** (presenter Matthew Allsop, audiologist) had 85K+ subscribers and 5.5M+ views as of March 2026 — [Harley St Hearing](https://harleysthearing.co.uk/insights/matthew-allsop-europes-biggest-hearing-technology-youtuber/)
- **Dr. Cliff Olson** (audiologist, Applied Hearing Solutions, Phoenix): a HearingTracker page cites 260k+ subscribers, 800+ videos, and 42M+ views, while a newer source cites 430k+ subscribers. He monetizes indirectly through a provider network (HearingUp) and a partnership with Audigy to launch HearingLoss.com. No revenue figures are public; forum comments suggest the channel works as a low-cost advertising platform for clinic and network business [ANECDOTAL]. HearingTracker is closely linked to him, so it is not an independent source — [HearingTracker press release](https://hearingtracker.com/press-releases/hearingup-is-the-new-home-of-exclusive-dr-cliff-content-and-the-new-hearingup-provider-network); [Audigy + HearingLoss.com](https://www.hearingtracker.com/de/stories/press-releases/audigy-and-dr-cliff-s-hearingup-network-join-forces-to-launch-hearingloss-com); [Harley St Hearing](https://harleysthearing.co.uk/blog/youtube-channels-hearing-aid-user-needs/); [HearingTracker forum](https://forum.hearingtracker.com/t/dr-cliff-and-audigy/75321?page=2)
- **Audiology Associates UK** (run by an audiologist and dispenser) has about 615k subscribers. VidIQ estimates about $8.4k/month in YouTube earnings, which is a third-party estimate, not reported revenue — [VidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@audiologyassociatesuk/about/index.md)
- **Soundly (HearSoundly)** has a YouTube channel run by "a team of hearing aid users and audiologists." Its growth numbers are paywalled — [ThoughtLeaders](https://app.thoughtleaders.io/youtube/hearsoundly)
- Treble Health (tinnitus) credits YouTube as a key driver of patient trust and growth, per podcast title and summary only — [Business of Hearing podcast](https://directory.libsyn.com/episode/index/show/businessofhearing/id/37560300)
- A directory lists 20+ hearing-aid YouTube channels as of 2026 — [Feedspot](https://videos.feedspot.com/hearing_aid_youtube_channels)

### Inferences
- In this niche, the dominant monetization for creators seems to be lead generation for clinics and provider networks, not pure affiliate commissions. Prescription hearing aids are sold through clinics, while OTC devices have affiliate potential.
- An AI-automated newcomer would compete against audiologist-authored video, a lab-tested database (HearAdvisor), and an active forum community. Differentiation would need a credentialed reviewer, real device testing, or a unique data or tool angle.

### Gaps
- No public traffic or revenue numbers were found for any small or independent hearing-aid or senior-health website, and no case study of a small hearing-aid affiliate site, successful or failed.
- HearingTracker's traffic and revenue were not found. Third-party SEO tool estimates (Ahrefs or Semrush) were not checked.

## 4. Hybrid models that work now

### Takeaway
The evidence favors models where AI is a production aid behind something Google and users cannot get elsewhere: first-hand testing, credentialed experts, proprietary data or tools, and off-Google channels (YouTube, brand, newsletter). Programmatic SEO survives when each page carries unique, useful data and fails when it swaps keywords into templates. YouTube and brand building were the documented route back for a crushed review site.

### Cited Findings
- **HouseFresh recovery [CASE]:** After about 2 years, a 2-person, non-technical team announced in October 2025 that Google visibility exceeded its pre-HCU peak. The team credited the growth of its YouTube channel and a Linus Tech Tips collaboration that reached "millions" and lifted brand searches, plus design, UX, and content changes. Navarro said no single change explains it and partly credited luck. Similar sites began recovering around the June 2025 core update — [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/housefresh-achieves-notable-traffic-recovery-after-google-algorithm-impacts-2/); [Unscripted SEO podcast](https://the-unscripted-seo-interview-podcast.castos.com/episodes/gisele-navarro-on-rebuilding-housefresh-after-google-reddit-ai-bots-and-the-2-minute-visitor)
- **Programmatic SEO:** Zapier is cited as still drawing heavy traffic from about 50k integration pages. Sources agree that pages with unique data hold up better than templated keyword swaps, and that Google's scaled-content policy targets page value, not production method [VENDOR/blog] — [alexcloudstar](https://alexcloudstar.com/md/blog/programmatic-seo-2026-indie-hackers/); [SEJ, good vs bad pSEO](https://searchenginejournal.com/good-vs-bad-pseo-learnings-from-a-decade-of-building-scalable-content-engines/594204); [SEJ, crawl economics](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/scaled-ai-content-often-fails-googles-crawl-economics-explain-why/)
- A programmatic site deindexed for low quality (no manual action) later recovered after rebuilding pages [CASE, single] — [SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/why-website-deindexed-by-google-for-programmatic-seo-bounced-back/552179/)
- The lab-plus-hands-on model (HearAdvisor and HearingTracker) is a working example of a "product spec and test database" in this exact niche — [HearingTracker](https://www.hearingtracker.com/expert-vetting-our-hearing-aid-review-process)
- Empire Flippers says buyers view sites convertible to newsletters, digital products, SaaS, or e-commerce as lower risk than pure affiliate or display sites — [Empire Flippers](https://empireflippers.com/you-can-sell-your-business-for-life-changing-money-in-2025-heres-the-secret/)
- Pinterest-focused guest on Niche Pursuits: AI content that genuinely serves users is unlikely to be penalized, while spammy AI content won't survive [ANECDOTAL opinion] — [Niche Pursuits](https://www.nichepursuits.com/carly-campbell-pinterest/)

### Inferences
- A defensible version of an AI-assisted hearing-aid site might combine an AI-maintained spec and price database or comparison tool, human audiologist review and bylines, real device testing (even limited), and a YouTube or newsletter channel that builds brand search. This is a reasoned synthesis, not a tested formula.
- Data-backed evidence that "AI-assisted + human expert" beats "fully AI" is mostly indirect: the documented failures involve scaled AI content without editorial review.

### Gaps
- No controlled comparison of AI-assisted+expert vs fully AI sites with traffic outcomes was found.
- No newsletter-first or YouTube-first income data was found for senior-health or hearing niches.

## 5. Common failure reasons

### Takeaway
Documented failure modes are: scaled thin or derivative AI content (manual actions, deindexing, Mount AI decay); no brand, entity, or E-E-A-T signals, which hurt even honest small sites; single-channel dependence on Google; and erosion of informational clicks by AI Overviews. Evidence on affiliate program bans and cost overruns was not found.

### Cited Findings
- Scaled or copied AI content leads to a manual penalty and near-total traffic loss (Causal) — [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/ai-powered-seo-heist-copying-website-loses-significant-google-traffic/)
- Mass AI content without editorial input is the explicit target of Google's scaled content abuse policy (March 2024), its manual actions (June 2025), and the March 2026 spam update — [SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/scaled-ai-content-often-fails-googles-crawl-economics-explain-why/); [SimilarLabs](https://similarlabs.com/blog/google-march-2026-core-update-ai-content)
- About 2% of monetized content sites were deindexed in March 2024, mostly AI-heavy — [Originality.ai](https://originality.ai/blog/google-march-2024-manual-action-ai-update-list)
- Self-promotional listicles and risky AI tactics drove 40-95% declines in early 2026 — [Lily Ray](https://lilyraynyc.substack.com/p/it-works-until-it-doesnt-ai-content-risks)
- Lack of brand signals versus big publishers (HouseFresh vs The Spruce) — [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/review-site-google-traffic-affiliate-seo-content-440143)
- "Why are so many affiliate sites losing organic traffic?" is a Search Engine Land analysis; it was not fetched — [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/affiliate-sites-losing-organic-traffic-443792)
