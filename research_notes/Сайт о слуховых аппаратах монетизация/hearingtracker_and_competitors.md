# HearingTracker business model and competitive landscape (US/English hearing-aid content, 2025-2026)

Research date: 2026-11-26. "Confirmed" means a primary or first-party source says it. "Estimate" means a third-party tool or an inference. Search was run through a general web search tool, not live Google, so the SERP positions below are proxies and were not verified in Google.

## 1. HearingTracker revenue streams, company facts, history

### Takeaway
HearingTracker is a small, audiologist-founded independent publisher. It has been "independent since 2013", is based in Austin, TX, and was founded by Abram Bailey, AuD. By its own disclosure it earns money from four sources: affiliate commissions, its own shop, advertising (display, sponsored content, sponsored video, newsletter, research), and, through the sister company HearAdvisor, fixed fees from manufacturers for lab testing plus badge licensing. No revenue figure has been published. Third-party aggregators put revenue under $5M.

### Cited Findings
**Revenue streams (confirmed, first-party):**
- The About page lists revenue as affiliate commissions on purchases through its links, sales in the HearingTracker Shop, advertising, and fixed fees that manufacturers pay HearAdvisor for public lab testing. The page says the fee pays for the testing, not the result. It does not mention a clinic network or paid sponsorship. — [HearingTracker About](https://www.hearingtracker.com/about)
- HearAdvisor's own revenue comes from four sources: public lab-testing fees for some lower-priority products, "Expert Choice" badge licensing, optional sponsored video packages, and retailer affiliate links, which are added only after testing. Fees "may affect whether or when a lower-priority product is evaluated", but they do not buy scores. — [HearingTracker review methodology](https://www.hearingtracker.com/expert-vetting-our-hearing-aid-review-process)
- The media kit (2026) lists these ad products:
  - Website: display (desktop and mobile), popups, featured products and homepage features, labeled sponsored articles, press-release publication with optional newswire distribution.
  - Forum: display and popups.
  - Facebook groups: sponsored webinar and event promotion.
  - YouTube: sponsored video, video bundles, and a multi-platform package (video, a dedicated web page, and newsletter and social sharing).
  - Email: native and display placements in the consumer newsletter and in the "Audiologist Informer" newsletter for professionals, plus dedicated e-blasts.
  - Research: commissioned consumer and professional surveys with CapVal AG. 43 projects and 12K responses to date.
  - Rates are not published ("custom proposal", minimum commitment). Lead generation is not offered as a product. Clinic listings are not mentioned. Sales contact: Tony Schwartz. — [HearingTracker Media Kit](https://www.hearingtracker.com/media-kit)
- HearAdvisor also sells "Private Lab Services": third-party validation, product-launch support and competitive benchmarks for manufacturers. No prices are listed. Its address is Rockford, IL. — [HearAdvisor](https://www.hearadvisor.com/)

**Historical clinic and lead-gen product (date unclear, likely mid-2010s):**
- Bailey's LinkedIn post announced a pay-per-view "Local Hearing Aid Deals" advertising platform for audiologists: clinics "only pay Hearing Tracker when a visitor views your deal". The post cited 1.6M unique visitors in the prior year. — [LinkedIn, Abram Bailey](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/announcing-hearing-trackers-advertising-platform-local-abram-bailey) (via search snippet)
- An undated press release describes "Local Hearing Aid Deals": standardized discounted bundles from nearby audiologists, a "Personalized Hearing Aid Match" survey tool, and a directory of "thousands of hearing care providers". It claims "more than 200,000 people visit HearingTracker.com every month". — [HearingTracker press release](https://hearingtracker.com/news/hearing-tracker-helps-consumers-find-affordable-hearing-aids)

**Company facts:**
- Founded by Abram Bailey, AuD (Vanderbilt AuD), Founder and President. A press release dates the founding to 2013. I found no source that supports a 2010 founding. — [Hearing Health Foundation blog](https://hearinghealthfoundation.org/blogs/how-hearingtracker-came-to-be); [HearingTracker About](https://hearingtracker.com/about)

Bailey was then "founder and CEO" of the "Austin-based company". ### Inferences
- Revenue mix (inference): with about 123K monthly site visitors, the bulk of revenue probably comes from B2B sales to roughly a dozen manufacturers and OTC brands (sponsored video, HearAdvisor testing and badge fees, newsletter, research), plus affiliate income on OTC devices. Display ads alone at this traffic would likely yield low six figures a year at most. The aggregators' $1-5M range is plausible, but I could not verify it.
- The direction of travel: HearingTracker moved from a clinic-facing model (local deals, provider directory) to a manufacturer-facing media model with a separate lab. The 2026 media kit no longer sells leads or clinic listings.

### Gaps
- No public revenue, profit or funding data. I found no evidence of VC funding, so the company is likely bootstrapped, but this is unconfirmed.
- HearAdvisor testing and badge fee levels, affiliate commission rates, and shop product mix are not published.

## 3. Content types and defensibility

### Takeaway
HearingTracker combines lab data (HearAdvisor SoundScore), audiologist wear-testing, a 20-year community forum, an annual buyer price survey, YouTube reviews, news (The Hearing Wire), and a provider directory. The defensible assets are original measurement data, proprietary survey data that others cite, named audiologist experts (an E-E-A-T signal), and the community (forum plus Facebook groups).

### Cited Findings
- HearAdvisor lab method:
  - A KEMAR manikin sits in an 8-speaker ring that plays recorded scenes (restaurant, office).
  - Each device is tested at "Initial Fit" (77% weight) and "Tuned Fit" to NAL-NL2 (23% weight), against a Bisgaard N3 standard sloping moderate hearing loss.
  - The SoundScore runs 0-5 and ignores price. Weights: speech in quiet and noise 63%, feedback 15%, own voice 12%, streaming 10%. — [HearingTracker methodology](https://www.hearingtracker.com/expert-vetting-our-hearing-aid-review-process)
- HearingTracker reviewers wear each device for at least a week and rate up to 8 categories. The overall HearingTracker Score weights the SoundScore at 31%, battery at 13%, and most other categories at 9%, plus a price adjustment. — [same](https://www.hearingtracker.com/expert-vetting-our-hearing-aid-review-process)
- HearAdvisor publishes rankings of OTC and prescription devices. In 2026 the top SoundScores were Fortell AI at 6.0 (prescription) and Yeasound RIC800 at 4.7 (OTC), so the scale now exceeds 5. — [HearAdvisor best OTC](https://www.hearadvisor.com/best-otc-hearing-aids); [HearAdvisor prescription](https://www.hearadvisor.com/prescription)
- HearingTracker's buyer price survey is cited across the web. Third-party coverage of it reports these 2025 US figures:

  | Measure | Price per pair |
  |---|---|
  | Average | $2,694 (was $4,672 in 2018) |
  | Median | $1,999 |
  | Clinic, cash | $4,727 |
  | Costco | $1,674 |
  | OTC | $502 |

  — [Walnut Invest stats citing HT survey](https://walnutinvest.com/stats/hearing-aid-cost-statistics); [HT price survey page](https://www.hearingtracker.com/hearing-aid-prices-survey)
- Other content: the forum (since 2006, acquired in 2017), brand and retailer reviews (for example ZipHearing and hear.com review pages), the shop, Facebook brand communities, and the 2025 news imprint. — [Media Kit](https://www.hearingtracker.com/media-kit); [HT ZipHearing review](https://www.hearingtracker.com/hearing-aids/ziphearing-review-how-does-it-work-and-is-it-right-for-you); [HT hear.com page](https://hearingtracker.com/hearing-aids/hear-com)

### Inferences
- A newcomer cannot quickly copy three things: lab measurements (they need a KEMAR rig and an acoustic lab, a capex of likely tens of thousands of dollars or more, unverified), the forum's 20 years of user-generated content, and audiologist bylines. The price survey is a link-earning asset, and press and other sites cite it repeatedly.
- The independent-lab positioning also produces B2B revenue. Manufacturers pay for testing, badges and videos, which makes the content moat a revenue source too.

### Gaps
- The number of devices tested by HearAdvisor was not found ("dozens").
- I did not verify the size of the provider directory in 2026.

## 4. Competitors: who they are and how they monetize

### Takeaway
The landscape splits into four groups. (a) Independent expert publishers that monetize through affiliate links and B2B sales: HearingTracker/HearAdvisor, and Soundly until it was acquired. (b) Lead-gen and telecare sellers owned by manufacturers or payers: hear.com (WSA/audibene), Healthy Hearing (clinic directory), and since 2026 Soundly under Ear Professionals Group. (c) High-authority generalist and nonprofit health sites that live on affiliate income: NCOA, Forbes Health, Healthline, Verywell, ConsumerAffairs, SeniorLiving.org. (d) Audiologist YouTubers such as Dr. Cliff, AuD, who monetize through ad revenue, sponsorships and their own clinics. Consolidation is under way: industry players are buying content sites to feed their provider networks.

### Cited Findings
**Soundly:**
- Soundly calls itself a "hearing healthcare marketplace that helps consumers find products, compare prices, and find licensed audiologists". Co-founder Blake Cadwell started it as a personal research project and cites "200K+ monthly readers". — [Soundly About](https://www.soundly.com/about)
- A brand profile says Soundly was founded in May 2022 with 2-10 employees. — [Brandfetch](https://brandfetch.com/soundly.com) (via search snippet)
- It sold OTC hearing aids through a partnership with Tuned, an employer hearing-benefits company. — [search results referencing MedCity/HHM](https://medcitynews.com/2023/02/tuned-secures-3-5m-to-grow-hearing-care-platform-for-employers/)
- Acquisition (Feb 2026): Ear Professionals Group, the parent of UnitedHealthcare Hearing, EPIC Hearing Healthcare and AARP Hearing Solutions, bought Soundly. Terms were not disclosed. Soundly keeps its OTC affiliate relationships and becomes a referral channel that sends prescription seekers to EPG's provider network. It claims relationships with "15 million consumers". Amy Sarow, AuD stays on the team. — [HearingTracker news](https://www.hearingtracker.com/news/ear-professionals-group-acquires-soundly)
- Similarweb ranks Soundly as the site most similar to HearingTracker (100% affinity). — [Similarweb](https://www.similarweb.com/website/hearingtracker.com/)

**hear.com / audibene (WS Audiology):**
- audibene is a digital company that finds people online, pairs them with remote hearing experts, and coordinates fitting through local partner audiologists. In other words, it runs lead generation, a marketplace and branded hardware together. Sivantos agreed to acquire audibene in March 2015. — [yespress audibene](https://yespress.io/audibene); [hear.com success story](https://www.hear.com/news/press-releases/success-story)
- In the US, hear.com's network has over 2,000 state-licensed providers. It guides customers by phone, then fits them in a clinic, through tele-audiology or with OTC self-fit. — [yespress audibene](https://yespress.io/audibene); [Hearing Review 2015](https://hearingreview.com/2015/09/sivantos-introduces-hear-com-us-market-younger-demographic/)
- The research tool's own background note, which I did not verify, says Sivantos merged with Widex to form WS Audiology. I believe the merger closed in 2019, not 2024, but this also needs checking.

**Healthy Hearing:**
- Healthy Hearing calls itself the "largest hearing-focused consumer website and clinic directory in the world", with 5,000+ US clinics listed. It does not own or manage the listed clinics. — [Healthy Hearing via Trustpilot/search snippets](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/healthyhearing.com)
- Ownership and the monetization model were not confirmed: the About page refused the connection. Similarweb tracks healthyhearing.com competitors (Apr 2026). — [Similarweb healthyhearing](https://www.similarweb.com/website/healthyhearing.com/competitors)

**Generalist and nonprofit affiliate sites:**
- NCOA publishes "Best Hearing Aids in 2026" and "5 Best OTC Hearing Aids". Its top OTC pick is Jabra Enhance Select M3 and its best-value pick is Audien ION Pro 2. — [NCOA best hearing aids](https://www.ncoa.org/product-resources/hearing-aids/best-hearing-aids/); [NCOA best OTC](https://www.ncoa.org/product-resources/hearing-aids/best-over-the-counter-hearing-aids/)
- Similarweb lists NCOA at 85% affinity with HearingTracker. — [Similarweb](https://www.similarweb.com/website/hearingtracker.com/)
- Forbes Advisor reportedly received a Google manual action for site reputation abuse around Sept 2024, which hit sections including health. Google never fully confirmed it. — [SERoundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-hit-forbes-advisor-38147.html); [Sistrix](https://www.sistrix.com/?p=169359); [BuzzStream study](https://www.buzzstream.com/blog/forbes-advisor-analysis/)
- Forbes Health recruited volunteer hearing-aid testers through a public form. — [Forbes Health form](https://docs.google.com/forms/d/e/1FAIpQLSeCGMqvJDlOMzphJNhrzR4zUz5mPCuYdza_XK0LKuwpMOmQWg/viewform)
- ConsumerAffairs hearing aid buying guides were hosted on a local ABC affiliate's "reviews" subdomain, a parasite-SEO pattern. — [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/subdomain-leasing-and-the-giant-hole-in-googles-medic-update-322035)
- SeniorLiving.org publishes brand pricing reviews such as ZipHearing, MDHearing and hearing-aid costs in 2026. — [SeniorLiving ZipHearing](https://www.seniorliving.org/hearing-aids/zip-hearing/); [SeniorLiving MDHearing](https://www.seniorliving.org/hearing-aids/mdhearingaid/)
- Other "best hearing aids 2026" pages include audiologists.org and smaller affiliate sites (hearliferestored.com, bestguide.com). — [audiologists.org](https://audiologists.org/product-reviews/best-hearing-aids); [hearliferestored](https://hearliferestored.com/best-otc-hearing-aids-2026)

**YouTube audiologists:**
- The "Doctor Cliff, AuD" channel has about 439K subscribers according to a third-party tracker (latest listed video June 2026). That is about 4.5 times HearingTracker's 98K. — [SponsorRadar](https://sponsorradar.com/channels/drcliffaud); [HT Media Kit](https://www.hearingtracker.com/media-kit)

**Lead-gen economics context:**
- Hearing-industry trade pieces discuss online lead generation for clinics as a standard paid service. — [Hearing Health Matters 2019](https://hearinghealthmatters.org/hear-in-private-practice/2019/lead-gen-audiology-practices/); [WebFX](https://www.webfx.com/industries/health/audiology/lead-generation)

### Inferences
- The highest-value monetization in this niche is a prescription-fitting referral, where a sale can be $2-5K per pair according to the HT survey. The players that capture it are owned by manufacturers or payers (hear.com/WSA, EPG/Soundly). Independent publishers mostly capture OTC affiliate income (OTC average about $502 per pair) and B2B ads.
- EPG's purchase of Soundly suggests that a content site with about 200K monthly readers has strategic value as a funnel. One exit path for a new site is a sale to a hearing network, a payer or a manufacturer.
- Healthy Hearing is widely understood in the industry to be a clinic-directory business that sells clinic listings and leads. I could not confirm its ownership (possibly Demant) or its model, so treat this as unverified.

### Gaps
- No verified data on traffic or monetization for Healthline, Verywell Health, Consumer Reports, HearingLikeMe (Phonak/Sonova-affiliated), ZipHearing's business model, or the hearingaidforum-type sites.
- hear.com US traffic was not retrieved (a Semrush page exists).
- I could not confirm whether Forbes Health hearing-aid pages specifically lost rankings.
